Why direct-language evaluations produce stronger reports

  • Idioms of distress are preserved. Many Punjabi- and Hindi-speaking clients describe anxiety, grief, and trauma through physical sensations, family duty, or honor rather than clinical terms. An evaluator who understands those expressions can document symptoms accurately instead of losing them in translation.
  • Clients disclose more. Abuse, sexual violence, and humiliation are hard to describe in front of an interpreter — especially one from the same community. Removing the third party often changes what a client is willing to say.
  • Testimony stays consistent. For asylum applicants, a report built on the client's own words reduces the risk of translation discrepancies between the evaluation, the declaration, and later testimony.
  • Cultural context is understood. Joint-family households, immigration debt, stigma around mental health care, and caste or religious dynamics are recognized without needing explanation.

Frequently asked questions

Do you conduct immigration evaluations in Punjabi?

Yes. Amrinderjit Singh, LCSW is a native Punjabi speaker and conducts evaluations directly in Punjabi, without an interpreter, for clients in NY, NJ, CA, WA, MI, WI, IN, PA, and VA.
