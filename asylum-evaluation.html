An asylum mental health evaluation is a clinical assessment that documents trauma-related symptoms an asylum applicant experiences as a result of past persecution or fear of future harm. It is used in affirmative asylum interviews and in immigration court for asylum, withholding of removal, and Convention Against Torture claims. Amrinderjit Singh, LCSW provides these evaluations by telehealth in 9 states.

How an evaluation supports an asylum case

An asylum case often turns on the applicant's own testimony. Trauma can make that testimony harder to give: people who have been persecuted may present with flat affect, avoid details, or recall events out of order. A clinical evaluation helps adjudicators understand those presentations. Attorneys use asylum evaluations to:

  • Document the mental health sequelae of persecution — trauma-related, depressive, and anxiety symptoms, with validated instruments and DSM-5-TR diagnoses where supported.
  • Provide clinical context for testimony — how trauma can affect memory, sequencing, and emotional expression, which can bear on credibility questions.
  • Support exceptions to the one-year filing deadline — federal regulations recognize serious illness or mental disability, including effects of persecution, as a possible extraordinary circumstance.
  • Address severity of past harm for humanitarian asylum arguments and the risk of harm on return for withholding of removal and CAT claims.

What the evaluation does — and does not — do

The evaluator's role is clinical. The report describes the applicant's reported history, observed presentation, test results, and clinical conclusions about symptoms and functioning. It does not decide whether persecution occurred or whether the applicant qualifies for asylum — those are legal questions for the asylum officer or immigration judge. Keeping that line clear is what makes the report useful and credible.

Affirmative and defensive cases

Evaluations are prepared for affirmative applications before a USCIS asylum office and for defensive cases in immigration court. For court cases, attorneys should share the hearing date at referral so the report can be scheduled ahead of filing deadlines; 7-business-day expedited service is available.

Interviewing without an interpreter

Many applicants from India and the Punjab region describe persecution tied to political activity, religion, or caste-based targeting. Amrinderjit Singh, LCSW conducts those interviews directly in Punjabi or Hindi, which avoids the filtering and hesitation that interpreter-mediated interviews can introduce when clients describe torture, sexual violence, or humiliation.

Frequently asked questions

Can an evaluation help if the applicant's testimony was inconsistent?

It can provide clinical context. Research and clinical practice recognize that trauma can affect how events are remembered and recounted. The evaluation describes the applicant's symptoms and how they may bear on testimony; the adjudicator still decides credibility.

Is an asylum evaluation used for the one-year deadline?

It can be. Serious illness or mental or physical disability, including effects of persecution or violent harm, can be an extraordinary circumstance excusing a late filing. An evaluation can document symptoms during the relevant period.

What if the client has never talked about the persecution before?

That is common. The interview is paced and trauma-informed, and the client can speak in Punjabi, Hindi, or English directly with the evaluator. Clients are told they may pause or decline a question at any time.

Is a diagnosis required for an asylum evaluation?

No. The report documents symptoms and functional impact; DSM-5-TR diagnoses are included only where clinically supported.
